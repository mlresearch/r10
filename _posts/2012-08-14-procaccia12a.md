abstract: We consider the problem of selecting a subset of alternatives given noisy
  evaluations of the relative strength of different alternatives. We wish to select
  a k-subset (for a given k) that provides a maximum likelihood estimate for one of
  several objectives, e.g., containing the strongest alternative. Although this problem
  is NP-hard, we show that when the noise level is sufficiently high, intuitive methods
  provide the optimal solution. We thus generalize classical results about singling
  out one alternative and identifying the hidden ranking of alternatives by strength.
  Extensive experiments show that our methods perform well in practical settings.
