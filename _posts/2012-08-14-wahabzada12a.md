abstract: Understanding the adaptation process of plants to drought stress is essential
  in improving management practices, breeding strategies as well as engineering viable
  crops for a sustainable agriculture in the coming decades. Hyper-spectral imaging
  provides a particularly promising approach to gain such understanding since it allows
  to discover non-destructively spectral characteristics of plants governed primarily
  by scattering and absorption characteristics of the leaf internal structure and
  biochemical constituents. Several drought stress indices have been derived using
  hyper-spectral imaging. However, they are typically based on few hyper-spectral
  images only, rely on interpretations of experts, and consider few wavelengths only.
  In this study, we present the first data-driven approach to discovering spectral
  drought stress indices, treating it as an unsupervised labeling problem at massive
  scale. To make use of short range dependencies of spectral wavelengths, we develop
  an online variational Bayes algorithm for latent Dirichlet allocation with convolved
  Dirichlet regularizer. This approach scales to massive datasets and, hence, provides
  a more objective complement to plant physiological practices. The spectral topics
  found conform to plant physiological knowledge and can be computed in a fraction
  of the time compared to existing LDA approaches.
