abstract: Recently two search algorithms, A* and breadth-first branch and bound (BFBnB),
  were developed based on a simple admissible heuristic for learning Bayesian network
  structures that optimize a scoring function. The heuristic represents a relaxation
  of the learning problem such that each variable chooses optimal parents independently.
  As a result, the heuristic may contain many directed cycles and result in a loose
  bound. This paper introduces an improved admissible heuristic that tries to avoid
  directed cycles within small groups of variables. A sparse representation is also
  introduced to store only the unique optimal parent choices. Empirical results show
  that the new techniques significantly improved the efficiency and scalability of
  A* and BFBnB on most of datasets tested in this paper.
