abstract: Cooperative Bayesian games (BGs) can model decision-making problems for
  teams of agents under imperfect information, but require space and computation time
  that is exponential in the number of agents. While agent independence has been used
  to mitigate these problems in perfect information settings, we propose a novel approach
  for BGs based on the observation that BGs additionally possess a different types
  of structure, which we call type independence. We propose a factor graph representation
  that captures both forms of independence and present a theoretical analysis showing
  that non-serial dynamic programming cannot effectively exploit type independence,
  while Max-Sum can. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach can tackle
  cooperative Bayesian games of unprecedented size.
